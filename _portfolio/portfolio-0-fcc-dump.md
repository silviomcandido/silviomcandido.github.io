### Challenge
At each collision point of the FCC-ee, beamstrahlung produces photon beams carrying around 370 kW. Eight dumps are needed around the collider, each absorbing this power continuously, with tight limits on space, lead inventory and a maximum flow rate of 300 kg/s.

### What I did
- **Conceived the CUSP liquid-lead absorber**, iterating free-surface configurations until the flowing lead absorbs about 95% of the deposited power, and took it from numerical proof of concept (multiphase CFD, conjugate heat transfer, structural analysis) to integration in the collider complex.
- **Engineered the 300 kg/s liquid-lead circuit**: P&IDs, pipe sizing from flow and velocity limits, and the hydraulic architecture (flow distribution, pumping, inventory, cover gas). Took part in the HAZOP as the system designer.
- **Specified the requirements and interfaces of the loop components**, including the electromagnetic pump for a supplier manufacturing study, and wrote the system functional requirements for the FMEA, working with beam physics, radiation protection, materials, integration and ENEA Brasimone.

### Outcome
The liquid-lead concept is now the **baseline design for the FCC-ee beamstrahlung dump**.

### Tools
ANSYS Fluent, OpenFOAM, ANSYS Mechanical, FLUKA energy-deposition maps (with colleagues), Python.
